Six of the most requested packaging structures, drawn as proportional guides so your designer can plan artwork panels, and so you can visualise the structure before we produce an exact dieline for your product.

Straight tuck end carton (STE)

The most common retail carton. Both tuck flaps close from the same side. Specify W × D × L (width × depth × length). Typical board 300–350 gsm FBB.

Crash-lock (auto-bottom) carton

Base locks automatically when the carton is squared up. Ideal for heavier products, bottles and fast hand or machine packing.

Sleeve (with separate tray)

An open-ended sleeve slides over a tray. Premium feel for tea, chocolates and gift sets. Thumb hole optional. Tray is a second blank.

E-flute mailer box (roll-end tuck front)

The e-commerce standard. Made in one piece from E-flute corrugated, ships flat, no glue. Printable inside and out.

Pillow box

Two curved creases form a pillow shape. Fast to assemble, great for small gifts, cosmetics, favours and vouchers.

Paper carry bag (block bottom, twisted handle)

Standard kraft or art-paper bag. Specify W × G × H (width × gusset × height). Handle patches reinforce the top turnover.

How to use a dieline

  • Place the dieline on its own layer in Illustrator or InDesign, set the layer to non-printing and the stroke colour to a spot swatch named "Dieline" set to overprint.
  • Extend artwork 3 mm beyond every cut line. Keep text 5 mm inside creases.
  • Keep glue areas free of ink and varnish.
  • Print a paper mock-up, cut, fold and check panel orientation before sending files.
  • Export as PDF/X-4 with the dieline layer included but marked non-printing.
