## How the list was built

Four research passes searched the web for currently operating organisations, prioritising ones with a concrete, dated buying trigger (a factory expansion, product launch, rebrand, new outlet, published printing tender, awards season, AGM). Every row carries the URL it was found on. No company was invented; names that could not be confirmed were left out or flagged.

**Caveats to respect before outreach**

- 33 rows are flagged `verify` in the CSV (an unconfirmed city, legal name or manufacturing scope). Check the website or LinkedIn page before contacting them.
- Cities marked "(verify)" or "n/a" were not stated in the source.
- Regulator and directory sites (EDB, Tea Board, CBSL, IRCSL, JAAF, THASL) blocked automated fetching, so their member lists are referenced rather than copied. Opening those links manually adds several hundred more names; see each file's "How to find more" section.
- Hela Apparel Holdings and its subsidiaries were deliberately excluded (winding-up petitions filed August 2026).
- Triggers are dated; a tender or launch from early 2026 may have closed. Use them as conversation openers and evidence of print demand, not as open opportunities.

## Who to call first

Rank by margin and repeat volume, then by how warm the trigger is:

1. **Tea, spice, coconut and food exporters (file 01)** – monthly carton and label programmes, export compliance you can sell around, gift-pack seasons. Highest lifetime value.
2. **FMCG and pharma manufacturers (file 01)** – large recurring carton and label volume; longer procurement cycle; a plant visit and a quality file win these.
3. **Apparel and BOI-zone factories (file 02)** – hang tags, cartons and labels to buyer spec; volume is steady and price-sensitive; win with speed and compliance documentation.
4. **Banks, insurers, listed companies and state bodies (file 03)** – annual reports, branch collateral and formal tenders. Register as a vendor now for the 2026/27 report season.
5. **Hotels, restaurants, agencies and event companies (file 03)** – smaller tickets, fast decisions, and agencies resell you to dozens of brands.
6. **D2C brands, cloud kitchens, bakeries, cosmetics start-ups (file 04)** – small first orders, very high volume of leads, ideal for the website's estimator and WhatsApp funnel. Automate rather than visit.

## Outreach playbook

**Channel by segment**

| Segment | Best first touch | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Exporters, FMCG, pharma, apparel | Email to procurement or brand manager with a one-page capability sheet and an offer of free samples; LinkedIn connection | Plant visit with sample kit; formal vendor registration |
| Banks, insurers, listed companies, state bodies | Vendor registration form plus letter to the corporate communications or procurement head; monitor tender portals weekly | Bound dummy of a past annual report; pricing at three quantities |
| Hotels, agencies, events | Phone or WhatsApp to the marketing manager; sample pack of menus, standees and gift boxes | Trade price list for agencies; white-label delivery offer |
| D2C brands, bakeries, cosmetics start-ups | Instagram DM or WhatsApp with a photo of a similar box and the website estimator link | Free dieline and a 100-piece starter offer |

**Message structure that works in Sri Lanka B2B print**

1. Name the trigger you found ("Congratulations on the new Kotte showroom").
2. One specific product you can supply for it ("printed brochures and hoardings for the launch").
3. One proof point (a similar client, a turnaround, a certification).
4. A low-friction ask: samples, a WhatsApp chat, a 15-minute plant visit.
5. Link to the relevant industry page on the website so the prospect self-qualifies.

**Cadence**: touch 1 (email/DM), day 3 WhatsApp, day 7 phone call, day 21 case study, then quarterly seasonal reminders (Avurudu, Vesak, Ramadan, Christmas, AGM season, back-to-school).
